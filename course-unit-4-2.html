What Are Logarithms?

Definition: If ax = b, then loga(b) = x

A logarithm asks: "To what power must I raise the base to get this number?"

Example: Understanding Logarithms

Exponential Form: 2³ = 8

Logarithmic Form: log₂(8) = 3

"What power of 2 gives us 8?" Answer: 3

Types of Logarithms

Common Logarithm (Base 10)
log₁₀(x) = log(x)

Used in science and engineering

Natural Logarithm (Base e)
log_e(x) = ln(x), where e ≈ 2.71828

Used in calculus and higher mathematics

General Logarithm
log_a(x) where a is any positive base (a ≠ 1)

Base 'a' can be any positive number except 1

Important Properties

Logarithm of 1
log_a(1) = 0
Logarithm of the Base
log_a(a) = 1
Inverse Property
a^(log_a(x)) = x and log_a(a^x) = x

The 4 Laws of Logarithms

Law 1: Product Rule
log_a(xy) = log_a(x) + log_a(y)

The log of a product equals the sum of the logs

Law 2: Quotient Rule
log_a(x/y) = log_a(x) - log_a(y)

The log of a quotient equals the difference of the logs

Law 3: Power Rule
log_a(x^n) = n × log_a(x)

The log of a power equals the exponent times the log

Law 4: Change of Base
log_a(x) = log_b(x) / log_b(a)

Convert between different logarithm bases

Real-World Application: Sound Levels

Decibels (dB): Decibels = 10 × log₁₀(intensity)

Example: Concert Sound Level

If sound intensity is 10⁷ times the reference intensity:

dB = 10 × log₁₀(10⁷) = 10 × 7 = 70 dB

Method 1: Using Product Rule

Example: Expand log(2x)
1 Identify the Operation: We have a product (2x), so we use Law 1
2 Apply Law 1: log(2x) = log(2) + log(x)
3 Final Answer: log(2) + log(x)

Method 2: Using Power Rule

Example: Simplify log(x³)
1 Identify the Operation: We have a power (x³), so we use Law 3
2 Apply Law 3: log(x³) = 3 × log(x)
3 Simplify: 3log(x)

Method 3: Using Change of Base

Example: Calculate log₂(8)
1 Recognize the Need: We need to convert log₂ to a common base
2 Apply Change of Base: log₂(8) = log(8) / log(2)
3 Calculate: = 0.903 / 0.301 ≈ 3
4 Verify: 2³ = 8 ✓, so log₂(8) = 3

Method 4: Combining Laws

Example: Expand log(5x²y)
1 Identify Components: Product of 5, x², and y
2 Apply Law 1 (Product): log(5) + log(x²) + log(y)
3 Apply Law 3 (Power): log(5) + 2log(x) + log(y)
4 Final Answer: log(5) + 2log(x) + log(y)

Practice Problems

Problem 1: Convert to logarithmic form

Easy

Write in logarithmic form: 3⁴ = 81

Step 1: Identify base (a) = 3, exponent (x) = 4, result (b) = 81
Step 2: Use the form loga(b) = x
Answer: log₃(81) = 4

Problem 2: Expand using logarithm laws

Easy

Expand: log(5x²y)

Step 1: Apply Law 1 (Product Rule)
log(5x²y) = log(5) + log(x²) + log(y)
Step 2: Apply Law 3 (Power Rule) to log(x²)
= log(5) + 2log(x) + log(y)
Answer: log(5) + 2log(x) + log(y)

Problem 3: Condense using logarithm laws

Medium

Condense: 2log(x) + log(3) - log(y)

Step 1: Apply reverse Law 3: 2log(x) = log(x²)
log(x²) + log(3) - log(y)
Step 2: Apply Law 1 (Product): log(x²) + log(3) = log(3x²)
log(3x²) - log(y)
Step 3: Apply Law 2 (Quotient)
Answer: log(3x²/y)

Problem 4: Change of base formula

Medium

Evaluate: log₂(16) using change of base

Step 1: Apply Law 4: log₂(16) = log(16) / log(2)
Step 2: Calculate: = 1.204 / 0.301 ≈ 4
Step 3: Verify: 2⁴ = 16 ✓
Answer: 4

Problem 5: Complex logarithmic expression

Hard

Simplify: log₃(27) + log₃(9) - log₃(3)

Step 1: Evaluate each term
log₃(27) = 3 (since 3³ = 27)
log₃(9) = 2 (since 3² = 9)
log₃(3) = 1 (since 3¹ = 3)
Step 2: Combine
3 + 2 - 1 = 4
Answer: 4
