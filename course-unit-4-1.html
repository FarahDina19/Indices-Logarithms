What Are Indices?

An index (or exponent) tells us how many times a number (the base) is multiplied by itself.

General form: an where a is the base and n is the index

34
3 Base
4 Index/Exponent

3 × 3 × 3 × 3 = 81

Key Terminology

Base
The number being multiplied repeatedly
Index/Exponent
The power showing how many times to multiply
Power
The entire expression (e.g., 3⁴)
Square
A number raised to the power 2
Cube
A number raised to the power 3
Coefficient
The number multiplied by the power

The 8 Laws of Indices

Law 1: Multiplication with Same Base
am × an = am+n

When multiplying powers with the same base, add the exponents

Law 2: Division with Same Base
am ÷ an = am-n

When dividing powers with the same base, subtract the exponents

Law 3: Power of a Power
(am)n = amn

When raising a power to another power, multiply the exponents

Law 4: Power of a Product
(ab)n = anbn

Apply the power to each factor in the product

Law 5: Power of a Quotient
(a/b)n = an/bn

Apply the power to both numerator and denominator

Law 6: Zero Index
a0 = 1 (where a ≠ 0)

Any non-zero number to the power 0 equals 1

Law 7: Negative Index
a-n = 1/an

A negative exponent means "take the reciprocal"

Law 8: Fractional Index
am/n = n√(am)

Fractional exponents represent roots

Method 1: Using Laws Directly

Example: Simplify x³ × x⁵
1 Identify the Law: Both terms have the same base (x), and we're multiplying them, so we use Law 1: am × an = am+n
2 Apply the Law: Add the exponents: x3+5
3 Simplify: x8

Method 2: Combined Operations

Example: Simplify (2a³b⁻²)² × (3a⁻¹b)²
1 Apply Law 4: Distribute the outer exponent to each factor
= 4a⁶b⁻⁴ × 9a⁻²b²
2 Multiply coefficients: 4 × 9 = 36
3 Apply Law 1 for each variable:
a⁶ × a⁻² = a⁶⁻² = a⁴
b⁻⁴ × b² = b⁻⁴⁺² = b⁻²
4 Final Answer: 36a⁴b⁻² or 36a⁴/b²

Method 3: Fractional and Negative Indices

Example: Simplify 27^(1/3)
1 Recognize the Pattern: Fractional exponent a1/n means the nth root
2 Convert: 27^(1/3) = ³√27 (cube root of 27)
3 Calculate: ³√27 = 3 (since 3 × 3 × 3 = 27)
4 Answer: 3

Practice Problems

Problem 1: Simplify using laws of indices

Easy

Simplify: a² × a³ × a

Step 1: Apply Law 1 repeatedly (add exponents)
a² × a³ × a¹
Step 2: a^(2+3+1)
Answer: a⁶

Problem 2: Evaluate negative exponents

Easy

Evaluate: 5⁻²

Step 1: Apply Law 7: a⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ
5⁻² = 1/5²
Step 2: Calculate: 1/25 = 0.04
Answer: 0.04 or 1/25

Problem 3: Power of a power

Medium

Simplify: (x²)³ × (x²)²

Step 1: Apply Law 3 to each term (multiply exponents)
(x²)³ = x⁶ and (x²)² = x⁴
Step 2: Now we have x⁶ × x⁴
Step 3: Apply Law 1 (add exponents)
x^(6+4)
Answer: x¹⁰

Problem 4: Fractional exponents

Medium

Evaluate: 27^(1/3)

Step 1: Apply Law 8: a^(1/n) = ⁿ√a
27^(1/3) = ³√27
Step 2: Find the cube root (what number × itself 3 times = 27?)
3 × 3 × 3 = 27
Answer: 3

Problem 5: Complex simplification

Hard

Simplify: (2a³b⁻²)² × (3a⁻¹b)²

Step 1: Apply Law 4 (power of a product)
(2a³b⁻²)² = 4a⁶b⁻⁴
(3a⁻¹b)² = 9a⁻²b²
Step 2: Multiply the results
4a⁶b⁻⁴ × 9a⁻²b²
Step 3: Multiply coefficients: 4 × 9 = 36
Step 4: Apply Law 1 for each variable
a⁶ × a⁻² = a⁴
b⁻⁴ × b² = b⁻²
Answer: 36a⁴b⁻² or 36a⁴/b²
